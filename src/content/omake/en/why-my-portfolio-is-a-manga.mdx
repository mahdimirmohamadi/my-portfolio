Open ten developer portfolios and you'll see the same page ten times: dark background, glowing gradient, three cards, a "Let's talk" button. I've built enough landing pages to know that template by heart, and I didn't want to live inside it.

So this site is a manga. You're reading **Volume 1 of The Saffron Chronicle**.

<Bubble speaker="simo">I'm Simo, the narrator. I'm a tiny Simorgh, and yes, I'm judging your Core Web Vitals.</Bubble>

## Why manga?

Two reasons, both a bit nerdy.

1. **Manga reads right to left, and so does Persian.** The Persian version of this site isn't a translation added at the end. It's the *original printing*, and the English one is the flipped Western edition.
2. **Manga is black and white with one spot colour.** In my photo I'm wearing a yellow shirt, so saffron became the single colour on every page.

<SFX en="KA-BOOM" fa="بوم!" />

## The rules I set myself

- **No blue or purple gradients.** Flat colour, ink lines, screentone dots.
- **Motion has to earn its place.** Big moments (the cover, a boss battle, finishing a post) get the full treatment. Things you do all the time (links, the menu) stay fast and quiet.
- **Phones first.** On a small screen the manga page becomes a vertical webtoon strip, which is how people read comics on their phones anyway.
- **It must be fast.** The cover photo is inked by a small build script into an 8-colour image, and the home page ships almost no JavaScript.

```ts
// the saffron plate is printed a few pixels off, on purpose
const MISREG = { x: 4, y: 3 };
```

That misregistration is my favourite detail. Cheap two-colour printing never lines up perfectly, and that small flaw is what makes a page feel printed by people.
